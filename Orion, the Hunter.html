Orion

The Hunter stands on the southern sky every northern winter. Seven bright stars make him easy to find, and you can spot him from a city. Tap or click any star to meet it.

Hidden room: tap the three belt stars from left to right (Alnitak, Alnilam, Mintaka), or type "hunter".

How to find him

Look south

From December to February, face south after dark. Orion rises in the east in the evening and climbs high by midnight.

Start with the belt

Three stars in a short, tilted row are the quickest way in. Nothing else in the sky looks quite like them.

Follow the lines

The belt points down-left toward Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, and up-right toward the red giant Aldebaran in Taurus.

The sword and the nebula

Below the belt hangs Orion's sword. Its middle "star" is blurry because it is the Orion Nebula (M42), a cloud of gas about 1,300 light-years away where new stars are being born right now. It is visible to the naked eye in dark skies, and a pair of binoculars shows it glowing.

The myth

In Greek stories Orion was a giant hunter, so skilled he boasted he could kill any animal on Earth. In one telling, the Earth sent a scorpion to stop him. Zeus placed both in the sky on opposite sides, so when Scorpius rises, Orion sets.

Orion's meteors

The Orionids are dust from Halley's Comet. They run through October and peak around October 21, with a handful of fast, faint streaks per hour radiating from near Orion's club. Look after midnight, away from city lights.

Distances are approximate and sources vary. The belt keeps an order, if you know how to read it.
